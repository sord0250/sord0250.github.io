Salesforce Case Workflow

A dental practice needed a reliable way to track each patient's implant treatment from first consultation to final install. I'm building a Salesforce workflow that moves every case through defined stages, automatically creates the tasks each stage requires, and puts everything about a patient's case on one screen.

Problem

Why this project exists

Two earlier vendors had already worked in the client's Salesforce org, but the client wasn't happy with either one. That left a crowded org full of half-used objects and connections, while patient progress was still tracked inconsistently outside the system. The client needed something their team would actually use day to day.

Discovery

How I scoped it

I started by learning how the team tracks a case today: who touches it, in what order, and which tools they rely on. I then mapped that against their existing technology stack and what was already built in Salesforce, so the design fit how they really work instead of forcing a new process on them.

Decisions

Key technical decisions

  1. 1
    Audit before buildingI used AI alongside the Salesforce CLI to pull and review the org's existing metadata. That showed which objects, fields, and connections from earlier vendors could be reused, saving rebuild time and avoiding duplicate structures.
  2. 2
    Status-driven automationA single record-triggered flow branches on case status. When a case moves to a new stage, the flow creates that stage's required tasks automatically. It went through many versions in a dev sandbox before going anywhere near production.
  3. 3
    One screen per caseI restructured the record pages to combine related information (patient details, images, implant details, open tasks, and current stage) into a single view with a visual stage path, so staff don't have to jump between records.
  4. 4
    Where sensitive files livePatient files are health data, so where they're stored is a security and compliance decision, not just a storage choice. I laid out the tradeoffs of keeping files in Salesforce versus an external system (access control, cost, and how much risk sits in one place) so the client could make an informed call.
What I learned Key Takeaways

Consulting is as much about communication as configuration. The technical work (flows, page layouts, metadata) moved quickly once I understood the org, but the most valuable conversations were explaining design options and their tradeoffs in plain language so the client could choose. Inheriting an org from two earlier vendors also taught me to audit before building: understanding what already exists is the fastest way to avoid adding more clutter.

AI uses
Org audit

Paired AI with the Salesforce CLI to read through existing metadata quickly and summarize what was reusable versus what was leftover from earlier vendors.

Flow design

Used AI to pressure-test the flow structure and stage-to-task mapping before building it, catching edge cases early.

Client communication

Used AI to draft clear explanations of technical tradeoffs, which I then edited for the client's context.
